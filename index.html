National Rail Conditions of Travel

Valid from 30 July 2026 • 35-page edition

The current Conditions of Travel form part of the contract between passengers and the Train Companies whose services their ticket permits them to use. They set out passenger and operator rights and obligations across Great Britain.

CrossCountry procedure • XCTL-1001

Passenger Information During Disruption (PIDD) Local Plan

Issue 8 • August 2023 • 25 pages

This plan describes CrossCountry's processes for providing timely, relevant and consistent passenger information during service disruption. It includes responsibilities for Control, customer service, station interfaces and traincrew.

Open XCTL-1001 Issue 8 PDF ↗
Document control: The PDF identifies itself as an uncontrolled copy when printed and says it should be reviewed at least annually. Issue 8 is dated August 2023, so confirm the latest controlled version and current instructions on company systems before relying on it operationally.
Traincrew quick reference – Section 5.5
When PIDD starts

On-board PIDD provision begins when CrossCountry knows a customer's journey will be delayed or disrupted, or when the customer first believes they may not arrive on time.

Information from Control

Traincrew should receive clear, concise messages from Control to guide decisions and passenger information during disruption.

Initial announcement

The plan says an announcement should be made within 2 minutes of the train coming to a stop and being delayed, even if it is a holding announcement.

First update

The first update should follow within the next 5 minutes.

Ongoing updates

Further updates should be made every 10 minutes until the train is moving. Updates should be adapted to events and provided sooner if circumstances materially change.

After departure

Until the train is back on schedule, post-departure announcements should keep customers informed of the ongoing delay.

How to communicate

The Train Manager or Senior Conductor considers whether to use the PA, face-to-face communication or a combination, according to the circumstances and passengers' needs.

CSL2

The Duty Control Manager considers activating CSL2 when services are delayed, or likely to be delayed, by 60 minutes or more, but may activate it at any time if appropriate.

These points are a quick-reference summary of Issue 8, not a substitute for the full procedure, Control instructions, emergency arrangements or current controlled company documents.

On-board operational aide-mémoire

Train Manager Quick Reference

Cross-document signposting for common passenger-facing situations. Use the source documents and current company instructions for exact requirements and decisions.

Important: This is a navigation aid, not an authority to detain, remove, refuse travel, issue a penalty, promise compensation or depart from operating instructions. Escalate to Control / the appropriate manager where required.
Intoxicated or unfit passenger

Start with Byelaw 4 (Intoxication) and Byelaw 5 (Unfit to be on the railway). Consider safety, welfare, immediate risk and the relevant company process. Do not treat intoxication alone as an automatic conclusion about fitness to travel.

Sources: Byelaws 4–5 · NRCoT Part E

Abusive, threatening or disruptive behaviour

Byelaw 6 covers unacceptable behaviour. Byelaw 12 concerns compliance with reasonable safety instructions in the circumstances described by the byelaw. Follow incident reporting and assistance procedures.

Sources: Byelaws 6, 12, 23–24 · NRCoT Part E

Reserved seat / accommodation dispute

Check the reservation and ticket conditions, the relevant accommodation class and the circumstances. Byelaw 19 addresses reserved seats and accommodation; the NRCoT and CrossCountry Charter provide wider passenger terms and commitments.

Sources: Byelaw 19 · NRCoT Parts C–E · Passenger Charter

Passenger without a valid ticket

Distinguish compulsory and non-compulsory ticket areas, check the ticket and any applicable exceptions, and use the current revenue-protection process. Byelaws 17–18 address ticket requirements in the respective areas.

Sources: Byelaws 17–18, 20–22 · NRCoT Part C–D

Safety instruction / emergency equipment

Byelaw 11 covers general safety and interference with equipment; Byelaw 12 covers safety instructions. Apply the Rule Book, safety management system and current emergency procedures where relevant.

Sources: Byelaws 10–12 · NRCoT Part E

Delay or disruption – passenger information

Use the current controlled PIDD plan and Control messages. Issue 8 (August 2023) includes traincrew guidance on the initial announcement, updates and communication methods; verify that a later controlled version has not superseded it.

Sources: XCTL-1001 Issue 8 · Passenger Charter

Missed connection / journey cannot be completed

Check the applicable National Rail Conditions, ticket restrictions and CrossCountry Charter. Do not promise a specific alternative route or entitlement without checking the live circumstances and current instructions.

Sources: NRCoT Parts D–F · Passenger Charter · PIDD

Refunds and delay compensation

Separate a ticket refund from delay compensation. Check the NRCoT, CrossCountry's current Delay Repay terms and the reason / length of delay before advising a customer.

Sources: NRCoT Part F · Passenger Charter

Name, address and enforcement

Byelaw 23 sets out the name-and-address requirement in the circumstances it describes. Byelaw 24 deals with enforcement. These provisions do not make every member of staff an authorised person; check authority, identification and company procedure.

Sources: Byelaws 23–24

Accessibility and assistance

Refer to the CrossCountry Passenger Charter and National Rail Conditions for Passenger Assist and assistance arrangements. Confirm the passenger's actual needs and use current assistance processes.

Sources: Passenger Charter · NRCoT Part E
